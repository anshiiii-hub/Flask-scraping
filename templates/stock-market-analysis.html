Problem Statement & Objective

The Challenge

Financial markets are inherently volatile. Investors and analysts require a clear, data-driven methodology to assess a stock's historical risk and performance patterns beyond just its current price, enabling more informed strategic decisions.

The Objective

To analyze the historical stock data of a major company by calculating and visualizing key financial metrics. The goal is to identify trends in moving averages, quantify daily return volatility, and present these findings in an accessible analytical report.

Data Source

The dataset for this analysis is sourced programmatically in real-time using the yfinance Python library, which connects directly to the Yahoo Finance API. The primary data points include:

  • OHLC Prices: Open, High, Low, and Close prices for each trading day.
  • Volume: The total number of shares traded per day.

This method ensures the analysis is performed on up-to-date, accurate financial data.

Visualizations & Insights

Outcome & Applications

The final outcome is an analytical dashboard providing a clear, quantitative view of the stock's historical performance and risk profile. The visualizations of moving averages and volatility serve as powerful tools for technical analysis. These insights can directly inform investment strategies, aid in risk assessment for portfolio management, and provide a foundation for more complex quantitative modeling.

Reflection & Future Scope

This project was a valuable exercise in applying data science techniques to financial time-series data. The primary challenge involved feature engineering to calculate meaningful metrics like daily returns and rolling volatility. It highlighted the importance of clear visualization in making complex financial data understandable and actionable.

Future Scope: The analysis can be extended by building predictive models (such as ARIMA or LSTM) to forecast future stock prices. Another valuable extension would be to develop and backtest a trading strategy based on the moving average crossover signals identified in this analysis.
